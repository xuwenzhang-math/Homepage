A stationary integral varifold with unique tangent cone given by a cross cone

We call the union of two distinct intersecting hyperplanes through the origin a cross cone.

In \(\mathbb R^3\), set

\[ a=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\qquad h=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\log3,\qquad \rho(x,y)=\sqrt{x^2+(y-1)^2}. \]

Consider the following five surfaces:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{(planar region)}\quad S&=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:z=0,\ y\ge0,\ \rho(x,y)\ge1\},\\[6pt] \text{(half-planes)}\quad P_\pm&=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:y\le0,\ z=\pm\sqrt3\,y\},\\[6pt] \text{(catenoid pieces)}\quad C_+&=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:z\ge0,\ \rho(x,y)=a\cosh((z-h)/a)\},\\[6pt] C_-&=\{(x,y,-z):(x,y,z)\in C_+\}. \end{aligned} \]

Give each surface multiplicity one and define

\[V=|S|+|P_+|+|P_-|+|C_+|+|C_-|,\]

where \(|M|\) denotes the varifold associated with the surface \(M\). Clearly, \(V\) is a stationary integral \(2\)-varifold in \(\mathbb R^3\). Its unique tangent cone at the origin is the cross cone

\[ C=|\Pi_+|+|\Pi_-|,\qquad \Pi_\pm=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:z=\pm\sqrt3\,y\}. \]
